Inside Epicor Kinetic

Every module in the suite, what it actually does mechanically, and whether it is Epicor's own code or something they bought. Epicor's own pages are almost entirely marketing, so most of the mechanism here comes from partner documentation and from epiusers.help, where the people running it describe what it really does.

It is an ERP, not an MES

Manufacturing execution is one department of a suite that also runs your ledger, your payroll, your webshop and your field engineers. Comparing it to a shop-floor tool compares the wrong things.

“MES” means two unrelated products

Standard MES is a terminal that records what people type. Advanced MES is a separate acquired product (Mattec) with its own hardware that records what machines do. They share a name and almost nothing else.

Much of the suite was bought, not built

CPQ, FP&A, field service, tax, document management, BI, EDI and the webstore are all acquisitions or OEM deals. That shows up as seams: overlapping products, no published guidance on which to use.

Manufacturing

The part that matters most, and where the documentation is deepest. Everything hangs off one hierarchy: job → assembly → operation → material. Labour, scrap, costing, scheduling and the work queue are all projections of “employee + operation + quantity”.

Job Management

Base

A job is a header with assemblies beneath it, each carrying its own operations and materials. “Get Details” copies the part's Method of Manufacture — the BOM plus the bill of operations — onto the job as an editable snapshot, so a job can diverge from the engineering master without changing it. Jobs move Engineered → Released → Completed → Closed; only a released job reaches the shop floor.

Demand links (make-to-order vs make-to-stock)

Base

The link between a job and a sales order lives in a separate record, not on either one — created from the order via the Order Job Wizard, or from the job as a demand link. A job with no link is make-to-stock and receives into a warehouse; a linked job's output goes to the shipment. One job can serve several deliveries.

Job costing

Base

Four buckets — material, material burden, subcontract, and labour plus burden — each held as estimate and actual side by side. Estimates come from the resource's rates applied to the operation's estimated hours; actuals come from MES labour transactions and material issues. Costs sit in work-in-progress until a capture process posts them, and closing the job computes the variance.

In practice: roll up standard costs after a job exists and the job's estimates do not recalculate, so you get phantom variance that has nothing to do with the shop floor.

MES terminal — the shop-floor screen

Base

Packaged as the Data Collection module. A terminal usually runs under one generic account and workers identify themselves with an employee ID, so time is attributed to the person, not the login. Clock-in and clock-out bracket the shift with configurable grace periods, separately from job activity.

The worker picks job, assembly and operation, then starts setup or production — tracked as separate cost elements. Each start/end pair writes a labour detail record. On ending, they report quantity completed, scrap quantity and reason, and can flag the operation finished.

Concurrent activities

Base

A worker can have several activities open at once and the system splits their hours across the jobs proportionally. This is how one operator minding four machines gets costed correctly instead of four times their clock time — a non-obvious requirement that most simple systems get wrong.

Work Queue

Base

A prioritised list of operations at the terminal, filtered to current, available or expected work. Mechanically it is a projection of the schedule, so it is only as sensible as your scheduling data.

The gap: the Work Queue exists only inside the shop-floor console. Supervisors cannot assign work to people from the main application — there is an open enhancement request for it, and sites routinely build their own tooling. The material side has a queue manager; the labour side does not.

Material Queue and Shop Tracker

Base

The Material Queue is the handler's counterpart to the Work Queue: move requests to stage material at a work centre, picked off and confirmed. Shop Tracker is the supervisor's view of who is clocked in and what each work centre is running. Job, order, customer and shipment trackers are read-only drill-downs so a shop-floor user can answer “where is this order” without an ERP seat.

Non-conformance and DMR

Base

A non-conformance raised from the floor moves parts out of work-in-progress into Inspection Processing, which has separate paths for manufactured parts after an operation and purchased material at receipt. Failing inspection issues a DMR number and routes the material into a disposition process. Scrap and non-conformance are deliberately different things: one is waste, the other is a decision still to be made.

In practice: the non-conformance number is not shown in the interface. You find it in the reference column of part transaction history.

Advanced MES

Acquired

A different product, not a tier. Standard MES is a transaction engine that records what a person types. Advanced MES listens to the equipment: a purpose-built interface unit wires into machine signals — including non-OPC PLCs — with onboard caching so counts survive a network outage, and counts every cycle without a human tally.

Downtime is detected from the machine signal and the operator is then made to classify it, so the stop is recorded whether or not anyone reacts; it catches micro-stops nobody would ever log. Because runtime, downtime, cycle time and scrap are all machine-captured, OEE is computed rather than self-reported. Adds its own scheduler that updates from observed machine speeds, statistical process control on streaming process variables, meter-based preventive maintenance and energy monitoring.

Worth knowing: Epicor publishes no explanation of how Advanced MES integrates with Kinetic, and it brings a second scheduler that overlaps the ERP's own. Roots are in plastics, rubber, automotive and metals.

Scheduling — the capacity model

Base APS adds

Work centres are resource groups containing individual resources. An operation points at a specific resource, a group, or — with Advanced Planning and Scheduling — a capability, a named skill held by several resources, so the scheduler picks whichever qualifying one is free. Each resource carries its own calendar.

Capacity is finite or infinite per resource. APS adds start-to-start, finish-to-finish and send-ahead relationships between operations, lets one operation span several resources at once, treats material availability as a constraint alongside capacity, and provides Gantt boards for a single job, for multiple resources, and a what-if mode.

In practice: the finite flag must be set in two places, and the finite horizon is a window in days — beyond it the system silently goes back to scheduling infinitely. Scheduling type is sticky: switching a site between finite and infinite means pulling every job off the schedule first.

Quality — three different things

Base QMS acquired

Quality Assurance (base) is inspection processing, non-conformance, DMR and material review. Enhanced Quality Assurance adds revision-controlled inspection plans with defined attributes and pass/fail values, configurable sample sizes, skip-lot processing that lets good suppliers bypass inspection, and calibration plans — though its control charts are built by querying the data out, not by a built-in chart engine. Epicor QMS is a separate cloud product from ETQ covering CAPA, audits, document control, training records and supplier quality.

Thin spot: independent analysis rates non-conformance and corrective action as Kinetic's weakest area, and certificates of conformance are not described as a named feature anywhere. Corrective actions are normally chained to a DMR; raising a standalone one is awkward.

Engineering — methods and change control

Base PLM add-on

The Engineering Workbench is where a revision is checked out, modified, approved and checked back in — the check-out is what stops two engineers editing the same revision. BOM and routing together form the Method of Manufacture, edited as an indented tree, with multiple and alternate revisions per part. Jobs created after an approval pick up the new method; jobs already running keep their snapshot.

CADLink pushes BOM revisions forward from mechanical and electrical CAD. Real product lifecycle management is a bolt-on third-party system (PRO.FILE); Kinetic itself is product data management. A costing workbench runs rollups through the method with variance analysis before you commit a new standard cost.

Advanced Production, Lean and Kanban

Base Nesting add-on

Advanced Production adds batching — grouping parts or operations from different jobs into one physical run, which is what ovens, plating and heat treat need — plus co-products from a single operation and automated nesting for sheet and plate. Kanban supports pull-based manufacturing without work orders, triggered by part-plant minimum thresholds.

In practice: the kanban signal is edge-triggered. Stock that is already below the threshold never fires — it has to be above and then fall below — so sites starting from depleted stock see nothing happen at all.

Supply chain

Inventory is held per part, site, warehouse and bin. Almost everything else in this family is a workflow over those balances.

Inventory and costing

Base

A part is defined once at company level and again per site; the cost method on the part record is only a template for new sites, and transactions always use the site's own method. Evidenced methods are standard, average, last, FIFO, lot average and lot FIFO — you cannot change the method while stock exists. Separate inventory, purchasing and sales units of measure with conversion factors; an advanced option lets a part transact in two units at once, such as feet and pounds.

Lot and serial tracking

Base

A checkbox per part; once on, a lot must be supplied at receipt, at job issue and at shipment, with attributes like heat number and expiry. Serial tracking ranges from none to every recorded move, with serials selected at operation completion, again at receipt to stock, and again on each subsequent transaction type you configure.

In practice: genuine multi-level traceability needs lot tracking ticked on every subassembly and raw material, not just the finished good — and the lot tracker often surfaces only the first level, so full trace usually needs a custom query.

Cycle counting

Base

A four-level structure: a period is a date range, a schedule is one warehouse's instance of it, cycles are the individual counting days generated from the warehouse calendar, and parts are selected into cycles by ABC code, which Epicor can assign automatically from movement and value. The rule that catches people: the period must be no longer than the A-code frequency, or A parts get counted twice or missed.

Advanced Material Management

Add-on

Licensing it forces an input warehouse and bin onto every resource and resource group, and makes the system auto-create move requests at events — a purchase receipt, a non-conformance, a quantity completed at an operation. Handlers work a request queue; supervisors get a separate manager view showing everything including claimed and stuck requests.

In practice: selecting a request — or double-clicking it by accident — moves it into that handler's personal list, where it disappears from everyone else's view.

Purchasing and approvals

Base ARM add-on

Requisitions are entered and routed through configurable actions, with approvals that are push-forward only — an approver forwards, a later one cannot pull. The final action must be flagged “send to purchasing”, and the lines land in PO suggestions for a buyer to accept or reject.

Purchase order approval runs off buyer records rather than a workflow engine: each buyer has one limit and one approver, and tiers are built by chaining approvers. Advanced Requisition Management adds catalogue-driven entry, approval trees by account, part class and location, punch-out to supplier websites, and real-time budget checking.

Two traps: a zero limit means no limit, so set it to $1 to force approval on everything. And approval is granted for a specific amount — raise the order by a dollar and it needs approving again.

MRP

Base

A regenerative run purges all unfirm jobs and suggestions and reprocesses every planned part level by level down the BOM; a net-change run only touches parts whose demand or parameters changed. Output is unfirm jobs, new and change purchase-order suggestions, and transfer suggestions. Typical pattern is regenerate at the weekend, net change on weekdays.

The clearest mechanic: minimum on-hand is the reorder point and breaching it creates a normal suggestion; safety stock is an emergency buffer beneath it and breaching that schedules the replenishment for today regardless of what is already in flight. MRP treats the two added together as the target level.

Forecasting and the master production schedule

Base Smart IO add-on

Forecasts are entered or imported per part and date, and any sales order falling inside a configured window around that date consumes the open forecast — there is no one-to-one matching between a specific order and a specific forecast. Master production schedule entries drive job creation, and once those jobs are firmed they override the schedule so MRP does not create a second set.

In practice: an order that misses its ship date stays open and, as the window rolls forward, keeps consuming later weeks' forecast — quietly inflating planned demand. Statistical forecasting is a separate cloud product, not base Kinetic.

Shipping, freight and customs

Base Quick Ship add-on

Shipment entry produces a pack slip which can auto-generate the receivables invoice; master packs group several pack slips into one physical shipment. Advance ship notices are the EDI 856 generated at shipment confirmation. Quick Ship is the multi-carrier add-on: real-time rate shopping across a hundred-plus carriers, label and manifest generation, hazmat paperwork and customs filing.

Container tracking and landed cost is separately licensed — you record freight, duty and brokerage against a container and apportion it across lines by weight, quantity, value or by hand, and it lands on the receipt and into part cost.

Sales and commerce

Quoting and estimating

Base

A quote line can pull a method of manufacture, and the quote worksheet then calculates estimated material, subcontract, labour and burden per quantity break and applies markup to reach a price. Winning the quote pushes it to an order — by line, by flagging the whole quote won, or by pulling selected lines from order management.

Order management

Base

Header, lines, and releases beneath each line — a release carries its own ship-to, date, plant and warehouse, which is how blanket orders and staged deliveries are modelled. Backorders are created automatically on partial shipment. Promise dates come from the live production schedule rather than a static lead time.

Pricing and discounts

Base

Price lists defined globally, by product group, by customer group or per customer, each with effective dates, currency and quantity breaks, in two flavours: unit price lists and discount lists.

In practice: users report the hierarchy genuinely misbehaves — mixing unit price and discount lists applies the discount on top of the unit price instead of stopping at the first match, and discount lists only work correctly with percentages.

Product Configurator

Base

A rules engine: the designer builds input pages and rulesets that include or exclude options and subassemblies, set quantities, look values up in tables, read the database or calculate. The output is a configured part with a generated bill of materials and routing, recoverable later from a “smart string” carried on the part number. That method then flows into the job like any other.

Epicor CPQ

Acquired

A separate cloud platform with its own visual programming language. Kinetic data is pulled in as queries which CPQ then treats as ordinary tables. It can match a configuration to an existing part or create a new part revision in Kinetic and build its entire method dynamically, inserting operations and materials from the customer's selections. Adds 3D and augmented-reality configuration and CAD automation.

Worth noticing: Epicor's CPQ pages do not acknowledge the native configurator, and the configurator pages do not acknowledge CPQ. Anyone on Kinetic evaluating CPQ may be buying a function they already own.

CRM

Base

Contacts, leads and opportunities managed against sales territories, with ordered task sets attached to quotes, opportunities and cases. Cases are the dispatch point for service, carrying warranty status and contract.

Set expectations: this is a transactional sales tool, not marketing automation. Campaign records track campaigns; there is no email automation, lead scoring or nurture. Most manufacturers pair it with Salesforce or HubSpot.

Sales commissions

Barely there

A rate on a salesperson record that flows order to invoice. Splits across reps, or any rule based on customer, product group or target attainment, are not native — documented practice is user-defined fields plus custom business rules, with payout reported outside the system.

Commerce and portals

Magento

The webstore is Adobe Magento with a real-time Kinetic integration supplying customer-specific pricing, availability and account history. Three tiers: the entry tier is effectively the old customer self-service portal — view quotes, invoices, pricing and stock, pay receivables — while higher tiers add a full cart, punch-out, returns and marketing tools.

Finance and projects

Financials Core

Base

A general ledger with dynamic account segments and a rules-based posting engine fed by every transaction in the suite. Multiple books let one company run several charts of accounts at once in different currencies and reporting bases — statutory, management, IFRS — with separate closing calendars, consolidation and elimination across legal entities, and allocations that distribute cost across projects, departments and locations.

Payables and receivables include a bank statement workbench, a credit manager workbench with automatic credit holds, deferred revenue and expense, and consolidated invoicing. Cash management covers promissory notes and postdated cheques tracked to settlement, ACH and EFT, positive pay, and lockbox files loading straight into receivables.

Asset Management

Base

Assets are created from a payables invoice — supplier, amount, terms and payment all linked to the asset — or by transferring a serialised inventory item into the register. Depreciation by straight line, declining balance, fixed amount per period or your own formula, across multiple books, with ledger entries automated.

Multi-company

Licensed modules

Separate companies, some cloud and some on-premise, with intercompany trading transactions, multi-company journals, global customers, parts, suppliers and shared warehouses, central payments and collections including netting, global credit checking, and costing by plant.

Tax

Avalara

Kinetic calls Avalara per line on orders and invoices and gets back a per-line tax breakdown, with addresses geolocation-validated rather than estimated from a postcode. Exemption certificates and return filing are Avalara's too.

Financial Planning & Analysis

DSPanel

Pulls ERP data into a multi-dimensional model handling several companies, currencies and calendars at once, reachable from a web interface and an Excel add-in. Budget workflow uses approval hierarchies with owners; machine-learning models generate a first-pass forecast; a rules engine automates calculations; consolidation runs in real time with elimination entries.

Project Management

Base Advanced add-on

Work-breakdown phases with approvals, against which jobs, purchase orders, inventory transactions and time all post so cost rolls up. Billing on progress, milestones or defined stages; revenue recognised by percentage complete, cost-to-cost or units delivered. A scheduling board allocates people, contractors and equipment.

Advanced Project Management replaces the simple structure with configurable cost codes, adds six billing types within one contract, standards-compliant revenue recognition, time-phased budgets and the cost and schedule performance indices.

Service, assets and people

Field Service

Base plus an acquired one

A service call becomes a service job; technicians book labour, materials and charges against it, and the system decides whether the work is under warranty, chargeable to a service contract, or invoiced. There is a mobile app with offline capability.

Confusingly: Epicor also sells a separate cloud Field Service Management product acquired from Evron, and its pages do not reconcile the two. Establish which one a quote refers to.

Warranty, contracts and returns

Base

Warranties are defined against parts, product groups or serial numbers, extendable by a service contract; contracts carry service levels, automatic billing, expiry and renewal. Returns run under an RMA number through entry, receipt and disposition, the disposition deciding between scrap, a repair job, a replacement or a credit.

Maintenance Management

Base

Equipment records driven by maintenance requests that become maintenance jobs. Equipment plans support both time-based intervals and meter-based ones driven by recorded readings. Jobs can carry full operations and spare parts drawn from stock, or be run lightly as text plus user fields.

In practice: it cannot natively express “whichever comes first” across a time-based and a meter-based plan on one asset — you configure two plans and over-trigger. This is a light maintenance system, not a full asset-management one.

Human capital

Treat with suspicion

The weakest and least honest area of Epicor's public material. The HCM product came from a 2010 acquisition, its product URL now returns a generic index, and independent listings mark it discontinued — while partners still sell it as a module. Independent analysis finds no native payroll processing, benefits administration, talent management or employee self-service; payroll and time-and-attendance are delivered by partners such as ADP. Native labour capture is the shop-floor clock and a time-and-expense app, which is job costing, not workforce management.

Platform and data

How customers change Kinetic's behaviour, and how data gets in and out. This layer is genuinely strong — and it is where Epicor's portfolio overlaps itself most.

Application Studio and layers

Base

A browser-based low-code screen designer. Customisations are stored as named layers holding only the delta from the shipped screen rather than a forked copy, which is what lets them survive upgrades. Layers move between environments through a deployment workbench.

The wart nobody mentions in the brochure: a layer binds to a menu item. Open the same screen from somewhere else — a drill-down, another form — and you get the base version. Customising one form cascades into customising every form that opens it.

Business Process Management

Base

The server-side rules engine, in two kinds. Method directives hook a specific business-object call with pre, base and post stages, so you can inspect and change its parameters or block it outright. Data directives hook a table instead, so they fire however the row was changed — standard ones after the transaction commits, in-transaction ones inside it, where they can roll it back. Widget-based, with a custom-code escape hatch.

Epicor Functions

Base

Reusable server-side logic in libraries with typed parameters and their own security and release lifecycle. Built in the same designer as a business rule, but callable from rules, from REST, from scheduled jobs, from screens and from other functions — so logic lives once instead of being copy-pasted across directives. Libraries move staging to promoted with separate URLs, so unpublished logic can be tested.

REST API

Base

An OData service layer exposing business objects, processes, reports, queries and functions — the same layer the interface itself consumes, so anything doable in the UI is doable programmatically. Version 2 adds application keys with access scopes alongside user credentials, which is a materially better security model than version 1.

Queries, dashboards and bulk data

Base DMT add-on

Business Activity Queries are a visual SQL builder over the schema and the common currency of the platform — they feed dashboards, search, REST endpoints and external tools. Updatable ones gain write-back, either auto-generated for simple cases or hand-written as rule logic, which is how editable grids get built without a custom screen. The Data Management Tool does bulk load from spreadsheets by calling the business objects rather than writing to SQL, so validation still applies.

Integration — three overlapping products

Workato + Jitterbit

Service Connect is the legacy XML workflow orchestrator, still shipped, no published end of life. Automation Studio is white-labelled Workato with an on-premise agent that reaches inside the firewall without opening ports. Integration Cloud is white-labelled Jitterbit. Epicor lists them as related products and publishes no guidance on when to use which.

Analytics — also three

Add-ons

Data Discovery is embedded in-app dashboards built from queries, with click-through into the underlying record. Data Analytics is Phocas, cube-based, with paid modules for financial statements and budgeting. Grow is the acquired self-service platform with 150-plus connectors. There is no published decision matrix and Epicor's own framing is that they fill different gaps.

Security

Base

Role-based access granted by attaching security codes to menu items, business objects and records, with users inheriting the union of their groups' rights. Field-level security controls read and update per field. Per-table, per-field change logging captures who changed what and when, which is what evidences an audit trail.

Note: the compliance story is a whitepaper rather than a feature set — the audit and security primitives exist, but no named tooling for data-subject requests or erasure.

AI

Early

Prism, announced January 2025, is a set of domain-specific agents behind a conversational layer using retrieval over company data — building automated processes, generating supplier RFQs, parsing returned quotes, updating schedules. One variant is sold on outcomes-based pricing. No model vendor is disclosed anywhere in Epicor's material, which is a notable omission for anyone with a data-governance question.

How it behaves in practice

The things nobody writes in a brochure, gathered from the forum where Epicor's own customers help each other. These are worth more than any feature list — they are what the first year of ownership actually feels like.

The finite schedule quietly stops being finite

The finite horizon is a window in days. Inside it capacity is enforced; beyond it the system reverts to infinite scheduling with no warning. One user set a 365-day horizon and confirmed it planned infinitely immediately afterwards. The flag also has to be set in two separate places, and the scheduling type is sticky — a job remembers the type it was first scheduled under.

Customising one screen cascades into several

Because a customisation layer binds to a menu item rather than to the screen, any other route into that screen loads the uncustomised version. Users describe a domino effect where one change forces the creation and maintenance of several further customised forms purely to preserve the first.

Nothing converts the old customisations

The classic interface was retired in May 2026 and there is no automated converter from the old form-script customisations to the new designer. Custom forms, interface modifications, embedded dashboards and any integration that drove the old UI are a manual rebuild-and-retest. This is the largest hidden cost in an upgrade.

A late order keeps eating next month's forecast

Forecast consumption works on a rolling window around the forecast date with no one-to-one matching. An order that misses its ship date stays open and keeps consuming subsequent weeks' forecast as the window moves, inflating planned demand until someone notices.

Kanban never fires if you are already short

The replenishment signal is edge-triggered: stock has to be above the minimum and then fall below it. Start from depleted stock and nothing happens at all.

Supervisors cannot hand work out

The work queue exists only in the shop-floor console, and there is no way to assign work to a named employee from the main application — an open enhancement request, with customers building their own. The material side has a manager view; the labour side does not.

Dates that matter

Epicor is mid-transition on two fronts at once, and both have hard dates.

May 2026 The classic interface is gone. Release 2026.1 shipped without the old smart client. Everything is the browser interface now — this has already happened.
~Jan 2028 Final on-premise release. 2028.1 is expected to be the last version you can run on your own servers.
31 Dec 2029 On-premise active support ends. From 1 January 2030 it is security patches only — no new features.
Ongoing Release cadence is shifting from two named releases a year toward monthly continuous delivery, which Epicor has not yet reconciled with the tiered upgrade-deferral options it sells.

What any of this means for a shop-floor tool

Three things are worth carrying away. First, the job → assembly → operation → material hierarchy is the spine of everything — labour, scrap, costing, scheduling and the queue are all projections of “employee plus operation plus quantity”, and that is the minimum honest data model for any system in this space.

Second, concurrent-activity labour splitting — one operator on four machines, hours prorated automatically — is a requirement almost nobody thinks of and everybody eventually needs.

Third, and most usefully: the single biggest complaint from Epicor's own customers is that a supervisor cannot hand a specific job to a specific person from the main application, and that the queue is only as good as the schedule behind it. A mature, expensive ERP has an open enhancement request for exactly the problem a custom tool would solve on day one. That is not an argument for building everything — it is an argument for building the one thing the big system does badly, and connecting it to the big system for the rest.
