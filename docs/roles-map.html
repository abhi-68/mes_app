Thermal Corp MES

Roles on the floor

Everything the system currently shows and lets each person do. Screens are what a role can open; actions are what the server will actually accept from them. Drawn from the code, not from the plan — anything specified but not yet built is marked at the bottom.

Sign in — works email and password
One account per person. The role on that account decides everything below.

Three roles. Each one inherits everything above it and adds its own.

Worker worker1–4@

A person at one station. Sees their own queue and their own hours, and nothing they cannot act on.

Screens they can open

  • My work — the steps queued at their station, split into running, ready, and waiting, each waiting card naming what it is waiting on/my-station
  • Overview — every unit in build, its progress, and anything blocked/
  • Work orders — any unit, with its sub-assembly tree and per-step history/orders
  • Inventory — on hand, reserved, on hold, available. Read only/inventory
  • Alerts — a count in the menu, and one entry that matters: the part this station was waiting on has arrived, go and start/alerts
  • Timesheets — their own recorded hours/timesheets

What they can do

  • Start a step — issues the material and starts the clock in one transaction
  • Clock on to a step a colleague already started
  • Pause — closes the labour session, keeps the step open
  • Mark done
  • Can’t continue — blocks the step against a reason code from the fixed list
  • Unblock once the cause is cleared
  • Log scrap or rework, again against a reason code
  • Clear an alert once they have acted on it — starting the step clears it by itself

Only at their own station, and only when nothing is in the way. Recorded times cannot be edited by the person who recorded them.

Supervisor supervisor@

Runs the floor. Everything a worker has, across every station, plus the two screens that exist to answer “why is this late”.

Adds — screens

  • What’s waiting — every step that cannot start and the thing holding it: a sub-assembly that has not delivered, stock that is short, or a quality hold. Separates work that is genuinely stuck from work simply queued behind its own predecessor/waiting
  • Reports — average time per step, per station and per worker against estimate, plus a scrap and rework Pareto/reports
  • Raise a work order — releasing one creates every step and every sub-assembly order from the product’s routing and parts list/admin/work-orders
  • All stations — the same queue screen, unfiltered/my-station
  • Alerts — blocked steps as they happen, plus material shortages and steps running past twice their estimate, worked out fresh on each visit/alerts
  • Everyone’s timesheets/timesheets

Adds — actions

  • Act on any station’s steps — start, complete, block or unblock anywhere, for cover and for clearing jams
  • Reopen a completed step
  • Correct a time entry — written as a separate adjustment with their name on it; the original is never overwritten
  • Add a customer

Inherits from Worker

  • Every screen and every step action above, unrestricted by station
Admin admin@

Sets the system up. This is where a different process per product gets defined — as data, without anyone writing code.

Adds — Setup

  • Products & processes — create an item, then build its routing step by step and its bill of materials line by line, including which step consumes which part/admin/products
  • People & stations — add workers, supervisors and admins, set the station each person works at, and manage the station list/admin/people
  • Reason codes — the fixed lists people pick from when something is blocked, scrapped or reworked. Fixed lists are what make these countable later/admin/reason-codes
  • Setup home — the four configuration areas with their counts/admin

Adds — actions

  • Create a product, purchased or made in-house
  • Add, reorder and remove routing steps
  • Add and remove bill-of-material lines
  • Create a station or retire one
  • Create a person, set their role and station, deactivate them
  • Create a reason code or retire one

Inherits from Supervisor

  • Every screen and action above, including all step actions at every station

The loop everything runs on

What happens to one step

Every action in the columns above is a move on this path. The checks at the start are the reason a worker cannot quietly begin something that is not ready.

Released
Step exists
Created from the product’s routing when the order was released, with its own material requirement.
Checked
Is it ready?
Predecessors finished, sub-assemblies delivered, no quality hold, material available.
Worker taps start
Running
Material issued and the clock started in one transaction. Tapping twice does not consume twice.
Worker taps done
Complete
Hours recorded from the clock. Progress rolls up the sub-assembly tree by expected minutes.
If not ready
The step says why
“Short 1 unit of Welded Casing Frame — WO-1001-01 is at ‘Fit base rail & lifting lugs’”. Start is refused by the server, not merely greyed out.
If something goes wrong
Can’t continue
Blocked against a reason code, visible on the supervisor’s overview until someone clears it. Scrap and rework are logged the same way.
Afterwards
Reopen or correct
A supervisor can reopen a finished step or correct a time entry. Both are written as new audited records beside the original.

Permission is checked on the server, every time

Hiding a button is not a control. Each action re-reads the signed-in user and refuses anything outside their role or their station before it touches a record — authorizeTask for step actions, requireRole for setup. A crafted request from a signed-in worker gets the same answer as the hidden button would have.

Who gets told what

Alerts

Two mechanisms, because two different things get called an alert and they do not behave alike. Nothing is emailed or texted — the count in the menu is the whole delivery mechanism, which is a deliberate first step rather than an omission.

To the station
Your part has arrived
Raised the moment a sub-assembly is finished and handed to the order waiting on it. Goes to that station alone. This is the one that recovers hours: everyone builds alerts that tell a manager something broke, and the time actually lost is between a blocker clearing and the person waiting finding out. Clears itself when the step is started.
To supervision
A step was blocked
Written the moment a worker taps Can’t continue, carrying the reason code and their note. Closes itself when the step is unblocked, so nobody has to tidy the list. Blocking the same step twice does not produce two entries.
To supervision
Material is short
Not stored. Worked out from stock and reservations each time the page is opened, so it appears the moment a step cannot be covered and disappears the moment a delivery lands — with nobody clearing anything. Workers do not see it; buying is not their job.
To supervision
A step is running long
Derived from the clock that is still running, against the estimate on that step. Twice the estimate today, which is a placeholder — the real number is a supervisor’s judgement about their own floor and nobody has asked yet. A step stops being late the moment it is finished or paused.

What nobody has decided yet

Whether a blocked step should chase someone after twenty minutes or wait until the morning; whether that is a phone, a screen on the wall, or a line in a daily email; who gets chased when the supervisor is on holiday. Those are the questions to ask on the floor, and every one of them changes what gets built next.

Honest edges

Built, but no one can reach it yet

These exist in the engine with tests behind them, and no screen calls them. Worth knowing before anyone counts them as features.

No screen
Quality inspection
Report production, accept, send to rework, scrap, place and release a hold — the whole disposition model is built and tested, and a hold set in the database correctly blocks the parent step. Nobody can inspect, fail or hold anything from the app.
Shortcut in place
Inspection is skipped
Finishing a sub-assembly now hands it straight to the parent order, because otherwise the chain deadlocks with no screen able to allocate. Output is supposed to be inspected before it is accepted; until that screen exists it is accepted on completion, and every record says so.
Not built
Drawings at the station
The table for work instructions and drawings exists and nothing reads or writes it, so a worker sees a step name with no document behind it.
Not built
Alerts that reach you
No email, no SMS, no wall display. Someone has to open the app to see the count, so nothing reaches a supervisor who has gone home or a worker who is not holding a tablet.
Not built
Scanners and devices
No barcode input and no event API, so nothing on the floor can report itself — every action is a tap. This is the hook a vision system would use later.
Not built
One station per person
A worker is assigned to exactly one station. Someone who covers two cannot be modelled, and a supervisor has to act on their behalf.
Not built
Approval on corrections
A supervisor’s time correction is audited but takes effect immediately. There is no request-and-approve step, and no rule against self-approval.
The stations, routings and assembly sequence in the demonstration data are a best guess from Thermal Corporation’s public Air Handlers catalogue, not their confirmed process. The permissions above are real; the factory they describe has not been validated.
